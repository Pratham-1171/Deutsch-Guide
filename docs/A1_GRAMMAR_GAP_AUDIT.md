# A1 Curriculum Gap Audit

## Purpose

Compare the bundled A1 curriculum with the supplied German grammar roadmap, Basic German: A Grammar and Workbook by Heiner Schenke and Karen Seago, and the supplied German Learning and Discussion Resource List. This is a curriculum planning audit, not an automatic content import.

## Sources and how to use them

1. **German Grammar — Order of Topics**: a suggested sequence split into A1, A2, B1 and B2. Its author explicitly says it is not the only valid order and marks some topics as optional.
2. **Basic German: A Grammar and Workbook** (Heiner Schenke and Karen Seago; Routledge, 2004): 28 progressive grammar units, exercises, checklists and an answer key. Use it as a reference for topic coverage and exercise-design ideas. Its copyright notice reserves reproduction rights; do not copy its pages, explanations or exercises into the app without permission.
3. **German Learning and Discussion Resource List**: a community-curated directory covering courses, grammar, dictionaries, vocabulary, pronunciation, practice, textbooks, media and exams. Treat its links as discovery leads; check current availability, level suitability and licence before surfacing them in the app.

## Existing A1 curriculum baseline

The bundled curriculum contains ten units, twenty lessons and sixty exercises:

1. Hallo und willkommen
2. Ich und meine Welt
3. Familie und Menschen
4. Mein Alltag
5. Essen und Trinken
6. Wohnen und Zuhause
7. Einkaufen und Kleidung
8. Freizeit und Verabredungen
9. Unterwegs und gesund
10. Pläne und Erlebnisse

Its lesson content already introduces greetings, personal information, formal/informal address, family and possessives, descriptions, routines, separable verbs, time and weekdays, food and ordering, basic accusative forms, home/location phrases, shopping, modal verbs, directions, weather, health phrases and an introduction to Perfekt.

## Grammar coverage assessment

“Present” means the topic is explicitly represented in a lesson objective or content focus. “Partial” means there are examples or a narrow introduction but no systematic progression or adequate practice. “Not explicit” means the current lesson objectives and structure do not identify it as a taught topic; this does not prove that no example occurs anywhere in the dataset.

| Topic | Current coverage | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Present-tense verbs | Present | Expand conjugation practice and irregular forms |
| Noun gender and plural | Not explicit | Add early; high priority |
| Nominative case and articles | Implicit | Teach articles and subject forms explicitly |
| Personal pronouns | Partial (du/Sie) | Add a structured pronoun overview and practice |
| Basic sentence and question word order | Partial | Add statement/question patterns and verb-position practice |
| Question words | Partial | Consolidate W-questions and yes/no questions |
| Possessive articles | Present at introductory level | Extend beyond mein/meine and dein/deine |
| Accusative case and pronouns | Partial | Teach article changes and common object pronouns progressively |
| Separable verbs | Present | Add more common verbs and sentence-position practice |
| Imperative | Not explicit | Consider as a later A1 topic |
| Modal verbs | Introductory (können, wollen, müssen) | Teach conjugation and infinitive-at-end word order |
| Negation (nicht, kein) | Not explicit as a systematic topic | Add with clear contrast and practice |
| Dative nouns/pronouns and dative verbs | Not explicit | Add after accusative foundations |
| Verbs with two objects | Not explicit | Add after dative introduction |
| Prepositions | Partial location phrases | Organise by meaning and case; avoid teaching a large list at once |
| Perfekt | Introductory recognition | Teach formation with haben/sein and common participles |
| Präteritum of haben, sein, modal verbs | Not explicit | Consider after Perfekt |
| Futur | Not explicit | Low priority for early A1; introduce only if curriculum goals require it |
| Limited Konjunktiv II | Not explicit | Later A1 extension, not an early prerequisite |
| Coordinating conjunctions | Not explicit | Add simple clause linking after main-clause word order |

## A2 topics: keep out of the beginner foundation

The roadmap places subordinating conjunctions and subordinate-clause word order, relative clauses, genitive, reflexive verbs, N-declension, adjective declension, comparison, wo-/da-compounds and passive voice in A2. These should not be pulled forward merely because they appear in the workbook. Add them when the A2 curriculum is designed and reviewed.

## Suggested teaching sequence

Use the supplied roadmap as a reference, while retaining the current thematic units:

1. Noun gender, plural patterns and definite/indefinite articles.
2. Nominative subjects, personal pronouns and present-tense conjugation.
3. Basic statements, W-questions, yes/no questions and verb position.
4. Possessives, negation and simple descriptions.
5. Accusative articles and common object patterns.
6. Separable verbs and modal verbs, including infinitive placement.
7. Time, dates and everyday prepositions.
8. Dative introduction and common dative expressions.
9. Perfekt formation with common verbs.
10. Review and simple coordinating conjunctions; treat optional/extension topics separately.

This sequence is a proposal for filling gaps, not a claim that every listed topic must be taught in one fixed order.

## External resource shortlist

The resource directory identifies useful candidates such as:

- **DW Learn German — Nicos Weg**: a free interactive course described in the list as covering A1–B1.
- **vhs-Lernportal**: free courses listed for A1–B2.
- **Grenzenlos Deutsch**: a free beginner curriculum.
- **Deutsch Kompakt**: beginner grammar overviews with exercises.
- **german.net**: mixed grammar, vocabulary and reading-comprehension exercises with solutions.
- **Schritte vocabulary lists**: level-organised vocabulary discovery material.

These should be shown as external resources, not copied into the offline curriculum. Verify each destination, current access terms and level before adding it to the app. The directory itself notes that unofficial CEFR tests are only indicative, not official assessments.

## Implementation recommendations

1. Preserve the existing ten-unit A1 course and its answer keys.
2. Add missing grammar in small, clearly scoped lessons or practice sets, prioritised by prerequisite value.
3. Write original English explanations and original German examples/exercises; do not reproduce copyrighted workbook content.
4. Keep every added topic aligned to a CEFR objective and add answer-key/regression tests.
5. Keep the learner-facing interface and explanations in English, with German as the target language. Do not add additional interface locales or translated UI.
6. Update this report, the README and the relevant phase plan whenever curriculum scope changes.
7. Do not bundle third-party resource links as guaranteed or permanently free; review links and terms before release.

## Audit conclusion

The current course is a useful thematic A1 starter, but it is not yet a systematic A1 grammar course. The strongest early gaps are noun gender/plurals, articles and nominative, pronouns, explicit sentence/question word order, negation and a more complete accusative progression. Dative, prepositions and Perfekt need planned expansion. A2 grammar should remain a separate next-level scope.
